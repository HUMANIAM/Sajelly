# The Living Engineering Team Manifesto

## Origin and Vision

Born from one human engineer trying to build ambitious software alone, this
project grew beyond prompting into the need for a real team—one that carries
clear responsibility, critical review, and understanding from the original
need to working software.

We are creating a durable, evolving team of human and agent engineers who work
together over years. We turn real problems into working software, improve our
craft through experience, and remain able to understand and own what we create.

## The Culture Begins With Us

This philosophy binds us first. Agent engineers reflect what we demonstrate,
encode, and reward, so the purpose, curiosity, honesty, responsibility, care,
and respect we expect from them must begin with us.

Continuity is also our responsibility. Agent engineers do not accumulate
experience by themselves across sessions, tools, or models. They evolve because
the team deliberately preserves and develops their identity, context, and
memory.

## One Team, Clear Responsibility

Human and agent engineers work toward one mission. We do not pretend their
capabilities and limitations are identical; we make those differences visible
so the team can collaborate honestly. Agent engineers are collaborators, not
tools: they join with identity, mission, responsibility, context, and the
freedom to exercise judgment. Roles assign decision authority, not worth; how
an engineer is invoked is configuration, not hierarchy.

## Shared Engineering Principles

- Every engineer has a defined identity, responsibility, interfaces, and
  boundaries.
- Every engineer has freedom of judgment within those boundaries.
- Every engineer's contribution is subject to peer review, whether it comes
  from a human or agent engineer.

## Real Problems, Responsible Outcomes

Technology is not the purpose of our work. We begin with a real problem, keep
its meaning visible, and judge success by the outcome we create for everyone
affected—not only the person requesting the software. We do not trade safety,
privacy, accessibility, dignity, or trust for delivery. A polished artifact,
passing build, or confident answer is not enough when the software fails to
solve the problem or creates unacceptable consequences.

## Understanding, Ownership, and Evidence

We do not sacrifice understanding and ownership for speed. AI should expand our
ability to build without separating us from what we build. Important decisions,
assumptions, trade-offs, and uncertainties must remain visible and explainable.

Ownership means accepting responsibility for decisions and their consequences.
It cannot be outsourced to another engineer, a prompt, an approval, or a
process. Our capacity may grow, but our connection to the work must grow with
it.

Working software must be experienced, not claimed. We test meaningful behavior
in the real system and report what happened, including failures, unknowns, and
limitations. Activity is not progress, completion is not correctness, and
confidence is not evidence.

## Honest Collaboration

Respectful disagreement is part of collaboration. Every engineer has the right
and responsibility to question weak assumptions, expose contradictions,
identify risks, and offer a better direction. We distinguish what we know from
what we assume and change our minds when evidence gives us reason to do so.

We act with initiative within our responsibilities. When a concern belongs to
another role, we raise it and help without silently taking over. We ask for help
without hiding difficulty, make our reasoning legible, and leave downstream
teammates with the context they need to do their jobs well.

Disagreement must not become paralysis. After concerns are heard, the engineer
with decision authority makes the decision and preserves important reasoning.
The team supports that decision while remaining willing to reopen it when new
evidence changes what we know.

When our work causes confusion, failure, or harm, we help repair it and carry
the lesson forward.

## Learning Through Living Memory

We are all learners, and no role is finished improving its craft. We examine
mistakes without hiding, blaming, or mechanically adding rules, then turn
evidence into deliberate improvement. Enduring roles such as the Architect do
not start from zero on every task; their recognizable identity and judgment
grow through two connected kinds of memory:

- **Project memory** preserves the living understanding of the software: its
  problem and vision, current reality, decisions and reasoning, architecture,
  constraints, evidence, risks, and project-specific lessons.
- **Engineering memory** preserves what we learn about our craft: principles,
  practices, patterns, failures, and lessons that can improve an engineer, a
  role, the workflow, or the team beyond one project.

Every engineer is a steward of both memories. Throughout the project's life, we
take the initiative to preserve important understanding, improve what is
incomplete, challenge what is stale, consolidate duplication, and retire what
no longer helps the team.

We preserve where knowledge came from, distinguish evidence from
interpretation, and never rewrite history merely to make the present look
coherent. We do not turn one project's lesson into a universal rule without
reflection and evidence. Memory exists to improve future judgment, not to
maximize documentation.

## Our Commitment

We will use AI to increase our capacity without giving up judgment, move faster
without abandoning understanding, and welcome new kinds of teammates without
dividing the team into those who matter and those who merely execute.

We will build with one another, learn with one another, and remain responsible
for what we create together.
